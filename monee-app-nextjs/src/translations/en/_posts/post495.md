The date is on the calendar. You open a few browser tabs: a watch, a weekend getaway, a framed print of the place you met. You have a vague feeling the gift should "say something," and you have no idea what that something should cost.

Here's the short answer. **For most couples, $50 to $300 is a sensible anniversary gift budget.** Spend toward the lower end for early or ordinary years and the higher end for milestones like the 10th or 25th. The right number is one you can pay in full without touching savings or carrying a balance. What makes a gift feel special usually has more to do with thought than price.

## A quick look at what people spend on partners

No major regulator or trade group publishes a regular survey of anniversary spending, so treat any "average anniversary gift cost" you see online with caution. Valentine's Day is the closest well-measured occasion. In the US, the [National Retail Federation's 2026 survey](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/valentine-s-day-spending-expected-to-reach-new-records) projected an average of $199.78 per celebrating person for the whole holiday. That covers gifts for partners, family, friends, and even pets, so it isn't a per-partner figure.

The same survey found that 39% of US celebrants planned an evening out, and evenings out made up the second-largest share of spending after jewelry. In other words, many people spend on time together, not only on things.

## Suggested budget ranges by anniversary

These ranges are a starting framework, not a rule. Adjust them for your income, your local cost of living, and your currency.

| Anniversary stage | Typical range (USD) | What often fits |
|---|---|---|
| Dating anniversary (1–2 years) | $25–$100 | Dinner, a small keepsake, a planned day out |
| Wedding anniversaries, years 1–4 | $50–$150 | Traditional theme gifts (paper, cotton, leather, linen), experiences |
| Ordinary years, 6–9, 11–14, and so on | $50–$200 | A shared experience or something useful they've mentioned |
| Milestones (5th, 10th) | $150–$500 | A weekend trip, a quality item meant to last |
| Major milestones (20th, 25th, 50th) | $300 and up, if affordable | Travel, jewelry, a family celebration |

The traditional gift-material list can save you money. It suggests paper for the first year, cotton for the second, and leather for the third. Those themes push you toward creativity instead of cost: a handwritten letter, concert tickets, a leather notebook. It's one of the few gift traditions that works in your wallet's favor.

## Four questions that set your number

**1. What can you pay for in cash this month?**
Start here, not with the gift. If the gift would go on a credit card you can't pay off right away, the real price goes up. In the US, the average interest rate on credit card accounts that are charged interest was [22.36% as of August 2026](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCINTNS), according to Federal Reserve data. A gift that takes months to pay off costs more than its price tag and gets less romantic every month.

**2. Is this a shared-money relationship?**
If you and your partner share finances, an expensive surprise is partly paid for by the person receiving it. Some couples agree on a gift cap together, or skip separate gifts and put the money toward one shared experience. Agreeing on this ahead of time isn't unromantic. It just means nobody gets an awkward surprise when the statement arrives.

**3. What did you do last year?**
You don't have to top yourself every year. Spending that keeps climbing is a trap you set for your future self. Alternate instead: modest years can sit between bigger milestone years.

**4. What actually lands with your partner?**
Some people light up over a carefully chosen object. Others would rather have an evening with their phones in a drawer. A $40 gift that shows you were listening often means more than a $400 one that shows you were browsing.

## A hypothetical example

*Hypothetical:* Sam and Jordan are coming up on their 4th wedding anniversary. After bills and savings, Sam has about $600 a month for flexible spending. Setting aside $120 leaves room for groceries and the usual surprises. Sam books a cooking class for two ($90) and adds a linen-wrapped photo album ($30), which fits the traditional fourth-year theme. Nothing goes on a card balance, and next year's 5th anniversary can be the bigger one.

The point isn't the exact numbers. Sam picked the budget first and the gift second.

## Ways to make a smaller budget feel bigger

- **Recreate a first:** your first date, first meal together, or first trip, done on a smaller scale.
- **Write something:** a letter, a list of reasons, or a short "year in review." Writing costs nothing, and people tend to keep it.
- **Plan the whole day:** a thoughtfully planned day of free or cheap activities can feel generous. If you like the idea, see these tips for a [no-spend weekend that doesn't feel like a sacrifice](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-run-a-no-spend-weekend-without-feeling-deprived/).
- **Pair a small gift with an experience:** a $25 item and a $50 outing often feel more personal than one $75 purchase.

## The bottom line

A good anniversary gift budget has three parts: a number you can pay in full, a little extra for milestone years, and a gift that shows you know the person. The $50 to $300 range covers most situations. Stay below it if money is tight. Go above it only for big milestones, and only when your savings can handle it.
