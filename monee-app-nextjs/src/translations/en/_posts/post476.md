For a classmate your child isn't especially close to, about **$15 to $30** is a common range in the US. Within that range, the right amount depends on your budget, your child's age, and how many invitations come in each year.

The consumer site Reviewed asked etiquette experts, and they called $25 the "sweet spot" for classmates and friends. Etiquette coach Jennifer Porter said [roughly $20 to $30 is a safe range](https://reviewed.com/parenting/features/how-much-money-to-spend-on-a-kids-birthday-gift). Keep in mind that this is expert advice, not survey data. Many parents spend less, especially for younger children, and nobody at the party is checking prices.

So the real question is which number makes sense for your family, not what everyone else spends.

## Start with a few questions

Before you settle on an amount, it can help to think about these:

- **How many parties does your child go to in a year?** One or two invitations feel very different from one almost every weekend.
- **How close is the friendship?** A child your kid talks about every day is different from someone they've barely met.
- **What do families in your community usually do?** Expectations vary between schools, neighborhoods and countries, so local norms are worth noticing. You don't have to follow them.
- **What matters most to you?** Maybe it's being generous, keeping things simple, teaching your child about thoughtful giving, or protecting a tight budget. Any of these is a good reason for your choice.

## A simple guide by situation

These ranges are rough and based on US norms. Adjust them for your own currency and local prices.

| Situation | Typical range (US) |
|---|---|
| Whole-class party, child barely knows the birthday kid | $10–$20 |
| Regular classmate or casual friend | $15–$25 |
| Good friend from school | $20–$35 |
| Very close friend or family friend's child | More, if you choose |

The same Reviewed article says gifts for a close friend's or relative's child can reach up to $100. That's an option, not an expectation.

## Why a consistent amount helps

When you pick a standard amount and stick to it, you make one decision instead of a new one before every party. It also feels fair across all your child's friends, and it keeps gift spending predictable.

To see your yearly total, multiply your usual gift amount by the number of parties you expect. Here's a hypothetical example: 12 parties at $20 each comes to $240 a year, before cards or wrapping. ## Ways to give well for less

A lower budget doesn't mean a worse gift. Some options:

- **Keep a gift stash.** Buying a few good gifts on sale ahead of time spares you last-minute store runs, which usually cost more.
- **Choose something to use up.** Art supplies, a book, a craft kit or a card game are usually welcome and don't add clutter.
- **Use gift cards you already have.** A store credit you haven't spent can cover part of a present. It helps to [keep track of your gift cards and store credits](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-gift-cards-and-store-credits-without-losing-money/) so they don't expire unused.
- **Go in on a group gift.** Some classes or friend groups pool money for one bigger present. If you're organizing one, a [simple split rule for group gifts](https://monee-app.com/blog/group-gifts-without-stress-one-simple-split-rule/) keeps it fair and low-stress.
- **Try a "fiver" party.** Some families ask each guest to bring $5 in a card, and the birthday child puts it toward one gift they choose.
- **Add a personal touch.** A handmade card from your child often means more to the birthday kid than a few extra dollars.

## What if other parents spend more?

You may notice that some gifts at a party cost much more than yours. That's fine. Children rarely compare prices, and most parents are glad their child's friend came at all. A thoughtful gift within your budget is a perfectly good gift.

If you're hosting a party yourself, the same thinking applies the other way. Guests' budgets aren't your concern, and some families write "no gifts, please" on the invitation. If you're planning one, see [what a kids' birthday party typically costs](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-to-spend-on-a-kids-birthday-party/).

## The bottom line

In the US, about $15 to $30 is a common and reasonable amount for a classmate's birthday gift. If you spend less, that's still fine. Pick an amount you can keep up with all year, use it for every party, and save larger gifts for close friendships.
