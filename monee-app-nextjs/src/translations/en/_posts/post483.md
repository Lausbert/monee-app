For most people, somewhere between **$30 and $100** is plenty for a baby shower gift. Where you land in that range depends mostly on how close you are to the parents-to-be and what you can comfortably afford.

If you'd like one number to start from, **about $50** is a typical amount.

That's the median from a [2023 survey of 1,000 people by Ally](https://ally.com/stories/spend/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-gift-for-any-occasion), a US bank. In the same survey, people who spent more than most gave around $75.

## A simple guide by relationship

Ally's survey also broke down typical (median) baby shower gift amounts by relationship. The figures are in US dollars:

| Your relationship | Typical amount (Ally survey) |
|---|---|
| Acquaintance or coworker | About $30 |
| Close friend | About $50 |
| Family member | About $100 |

These figures describe what people tend to spend. They aren't a rule.

If you're outside the US, treat them as a rough guide to how amounts scale with closeness. Then adjust for local prices and customs.

## Etiquette doesn't set a price

You might worry that there's a "correct" amount. There isn't one.

The [Emily Post Institute's guidance on baby shower gifts](https://emilypost.com/advice/baby-shower-gifts-and-registries), a US etiquette authority, doesn't give a dollar figure. It does say that "gift registries are not marching orders." No guest is required to buy from the list.

So the real question is a gentler one: what feels generous to you, without straining your budget?

## How to pick your number

Here are a few questions that can help you settle on an amount quickly.

**How close are you?** A cousin you text every week and a coworker you've met twice are very different. It's normal to give more to the people closest to you.

**What's already on your plate this month?** If several gifts are coming at once, a smaller gift is completely fine. You might also have a teacher gift, a [classmate's birthday present](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-classmates-birthday-gift/), or a housewarming coming up.

**Will you give again later?** Many people give a second gift when the baby arrives, or for the first birthday. If you will, you can split your budget across both.

**Are there multiple showers?** If you've been invited to more than one event for the same family, you don't need to bring a full gift to each. Etiquette guidance generally doesn't expect you to attend more than one shower.

## Ways to give well on a smaller budget

A modest budget can still lead to a thoughtful gift. A few ideas:

- **Go in on a group gift.** Pooling money for a bigger registry item, like a stroller or car seat, is a common choice. The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/baby-shower-gifts-and-registries) mentions it too. Your $25 can become part of something the parents really need.
- **Pick the practical stuff.** Diapers, wipes, and basic baby care items get used every day. Parents rarely have too many.
- **Make something.** Emily Post also mentions handmade gifts, like a knitted blanket, as a thoughtful option.
- **Add a personal touch.** A heartfelt card or a promise of help, like a dropped-off meal after the birth, can mean as much as the gift itself.

If you know the parents are weighing what to buy and what to borrow, this guide to [renting versus buying baby gear](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-rent-or-buy-baby-gear-a-use-time-test/) may help you choose a gift that won't sit unused.

## Registry or not?

Buying from the registry is the easiest choice. The parents get something they picked, and it avoids duplicates.

Going off the registry is fine too, especially for a handmade item or a "signature" gift you like to give. If you buy safety-related items like car seats or cribs, though, it's best to stick to the registry. The parents may have chosen those for specific safety standards or fit.

## What if you can't attend?

If you're invited but can't go, you aren't obligated to send a gift. That's according to [Anna Post of the Emily Post Institute](https://ezorigin.csmonitor.com/The-Culture/Family/Modern-Parenthood/2013/0827/Baby-shower-etiquette-Hint-it-s-not-about-the-gifts), writing in The Christian Science Monitor. A warm note sending your regrets is enough. You can still send something if you'd like, or wait until the baby is born.

## The short version

- **Acquaintance or coworker:** around $20 to $30
- **Close friend:** around $50
- **Close family:** around $100, if your budget allows
- **Tight budget:** a group gift, practical essentials, or something handmade

Whatever you spend, the parents-to-be are most likely to remember that you showed up for them.
