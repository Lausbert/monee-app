A career change is as much a financial project as it is a professional one. When you step away from a steady paycheck to retrain, job hunt, or launch a venture, your budget shifts from a tool for growth to a tool for survival. A six-month runway is the industry standard for a "safe" pivot, but the math is only half the battle. The other half is ensuring your financial systems don't trap you in friction when you need agility most.

### Auditing Your Liquidity
Before setting your runway, you must categorize your capital by "access speed." 

1.  **Immediate Liquidity:** Cash in checking or high-yield savings. This is your primary fuel.
2.  **Delayed Liquidity:** Brokerage accounts that take a few days to settle.
3.  **Locked Capital:** Retirement accounts or assets that carry heavy penalties for early access. 

For a career change, focus almost exclusively on Immediate Liquidity. Relying on Delayed Liquidity during a market downturn can shorten your six-month runway to four months without warning.

### The Career Change Scorecard

### Building the Runway: The "Clean Room" Approach
To budget effectively, stop looking at what you *usually* spend and start looking at what is *required* to keep the lights on. 

*   **Fixed Essentials:** Housing, utilities, basic groceries, and insurance.
*   **Variable Essentials:** Transportation for interviews, professional networking costs, and continuing education.
*   **Discretionary:** Subscriptions, dining out, and hobbies.

During a runway period, your Discretionary tier should be the first to be automated out of existence. Many switchers find success by "stress-testing" their budget two months *before* leaving their current role, living only on the projected runway amount and saving the surplus.

### The Switching Checklist
Follow these steps to migrate from a salary-dependent lifestyle to a runway-fed one.

1.  **Inventory All Subscriptions:** Use a tool to identify every recurring charge. Cancel anything that doesn't contribute to your health or your new career.
2.  **Standardize Your Data:** Export the last 12 months of expenses into a CSV format. This is your baseline.
3.  **Identify "Friction" Points:** Which bills are hard to change or cancel? Address these while you still have a steady income.
4.  **Establish the Buffer:** Ensure your "Immediate Liquidity" covers at least the first two months of the runway in a single, accessible location.
5.  **Test the Pipes:** Perform a small transfer from your savings to your checking to ensure there are no unexpected hold times or fees.

### Red-Flag Box: What to Watch For
> **Proprietary Walls:** Avoid budgeting tools that don't let you export your data. If you can't see your math in a plain spreadsheet, you don't own your budget.
> **Automated "Smart" Savings:** Be wary of apps that move money for you based on AI. During a career change, your spending patterns are erratic; automated logic can cause accidental overdrafts.
> **Gated Support:** If a financial service requires you to navigate a chatbot for three levels before reaching a human, it is a liability during a crisis.

### FAQ: Common Worries About Switching

**Is it better to use a dedicated budgeting app or a spreadsheet?**
Spreadsheets offer the highest portability and zero cost, but require manual discipline. Apps offer speed but often create "vendor lock-in." For a 6-month pivot, manual control is usually safer.

**What if I underestimate my "Essentials"?**
Always add a "Friction Buffer"—roughly 10% on top of your calculated essentials. Unexpected costs, like a laptop repair or a sudden health co-pay, are the primary killers of runways.

**Should I keep my savings in the same bank as my checking?**
While convenient, it can make it too easy to "leak" funds into discretionary spending. Keeping your runway in a separate institution can provide a psychological barrier that preserves your timeline.

### Final Decision
A six-month runway is not a suggestion; it is a shield. If your math shows you only have four months of "Great" portability and liquidity, consider delaying your pivot by eight weeks to bolster your reserves. Clarity today prevents desperation tomorrow.
