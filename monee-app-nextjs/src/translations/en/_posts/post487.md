Here's the short answer. For most people, a graduation gift of **$25 to $100** is generous and appropriate. Close family, like parents, grandparents and godparents, often give more. Friends, neighbors and coworkers can give less, and nobody will think less of them for it. Your relationship to the graduate matters more than the exact dollar amount, and so does what you can comfortably afford.

Here's how to choose your number.

## What people actually spend (average cost of graduation gifts)

The best US data comes from the National Retail Federation (NRF). In its 2026 survey, **39% of US consumers planned to buy a gift for a high school or college graduate**, and total spending was expected to reach a record **$7.2 billion**. Cash was again the top gift people planned to give ([NRF](https://www.nrf.com/research-insights/holiday-data-and-trends/graduation)). In 2025, 36% planned to buy a graduation gift, and total spending was projected at $6.8 billion ([NGPF, citing NRF](https://www.ngpf.org/blog/question-of-the-day/question-of-the-day-how-much-does-the-average-american-plan-to-spend-on-a-graduation-gift/)).

NRF's public summary page doesn't state the 2026 average per person. Secondary sources citing NRF put it at about **$177 in 2026**, up from roughly **$120 ($119.54) in 2025**. They also report that about half of respondents planned to give money ([Giftlist, citing NRF](https://giftlist.com/blog/graduation-gift-etiquette-giving-money-the-right-way); [NGPF, citing NRF](https://www.ngpf.org/blog/question-of-the-day/question-of-the-day-how-much-does-the-average-american-plan-to-spend-on-a-graduation-gift/)). Treat the 2026 per-person number as reported, not confirmed by NRF directly. Also keep in mind that a survey average like this usually covers everything one person spends on graduates, sometimes for several grads. It isn't the amount for a single gift.

**Bottom line:** if you spend well under $100 on one graduate, you're still in a normal range.

## Spending ranges by relationship

These ranges are general guidelines, not survey results. Adjust them for your budget, your family's traditions and what's normal where you live. Amounts are in US dollars.

| Your relationship to the graduate | Typical range (USD) |
|---|---|
| Parent or guardian | $100–$500+, or a meaningful item or experience |
| Grandparent | $50–$250 |
| Aunt, uncle or godparent (including a niece or nephew's graduation) | $50–$150 |
| Close family friend | $25–$100 |
| Friend or classmate | $10–$40 |
| Neighbor, coworker's child or acquaintance | $20–$50 |
| Graduate you're not close to (you only got an announcement) | A card, or $10–$25 |

Close relatives are at the top of the table because they're usually the ones helping with a big transition, like moving into a dorm, starting a first job or setting up a first apartment. Classmates and acquaintances are at the bottom because a thoughtful card is often enough. For gifts between students in other situations, see [how much to spend on a classmate's birthday gift](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-classmates-birthday-gift/).

## How much money to give a high school graduate

If you're giving cash for a **high school graduation**, use the relationship table above. In practice, that usually means:

- **$20–$50** from friends, neighbors and family friends
- **$50–$150** from aunts, uncles and godparents
- **$100 or more** from grandparents and parents, if that fits their budget

Cash is especially useful at this stage because many grads face costs for dorm supplies, textbooks or a first car.

## How much to give for a college graduation gift

For a **college (bachelor's) graduation**, amounts are often similar to high school, or a bit higher from close family, because the graduate may be about to move, start working or pay for a first apartment. A college gift for a **niece or nephew** commonly falls in the **$50–$150** range. Aunts and uncles who are especially close sometimes give more, or pair a smaller check with something practical for a new job.

## Adjust for the milestone

Not every graduation is the same size. Here's a rough guide:

- **Preschool, kindergarten, elementary or middle school:** small and fun. Think $10–$25, a book or a small toy.
- **High school:** the classic graduation gift. The ranges above apply here.
- **College (bachelor's degree):** often similar to high school, or a little more from close family.
- **Graduate or professional degree (master's, law, medical):** these are often smaller, more personal gifts. Many recipients are already working, so something useful for their career can mean more than a large check.

Here's a hypothetical example. Picture an aunt with two graduates this year: a niece finishing high school and a nephew finishing a master's degree. She might give her niece $75 toward college supplies and her nephew a $40 professional notebook with a handwritten note. Neither gift is "worth" more than the other. Each one fits where that graduate is in life.

## How much to spend on a graduation gift as a couple

If you're **giving as a couple** (one card signed by both of you), many couples give one combined gift that's somewhat larger than what one person would give. A rough guideline is about 1.5 times the single-giver amount for that relationship. For example, if one person would give $50, a couple might give $75. Close family who would each give generously on their own often just give one larger joint gift.

If **the graduates are a couple** (two people graduating together, or a married couple both finishing degrees), give each graduate their own card and gift. Or give one shared gift at about the combined value, such as something for their home or a dinner out.

If several relatives or friends want to pool money for a bigger item, like a laptop, here's [how to organize a group gift without fronting the cost](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-organize-a-group-gift-without-fronting-the-cost/).

## How much to spend on a graduation cookie gift

Decorated graduation cookies, cookie boxes and cookie bouquets are popular for classmates, coworkers and acquaintances. A reasonable budget is about **$15–$40** for a small box or a few decorated cookies. You might spend more for a large custom set from a bakery. Prices vary a lot by region and baker in the US, so check local bakeries before you settle on a number. A cookie gift fits in the "friend, classmate or acquaintance" range. If you're close family, you can pair cookies with a card and cash rather than making them the whole gift.

## How much to spend on sentimental gifts for a teenager

Sentimental gifts don't need a big budget. A handwritten letter, a framed photo, a scrapbook or a family keepsake can cost **little or nothing**. A personalized item, like an engraved bracelet, a custom photo book or a printed map of their hometown, usually costs **$20–$75**. Fine jewelry or a watch meant to last can cost more, and that's typically something from parents or grandparents. With teenagers, the thought and the personal touch matter more than the price. A modest sentimental gift paired with a small amount of cash works well.

## Cash, gift cards or a physical gift?

Each option has its strengths:

- **Cash or a money transfer:** Flexible and always welcome, which is probably why it tops NRF's list. You can make it more personal with a note about what you hope they'll use it for.
- **Gift cards:** Good if you know where they shop. Some cards go unused, so pick a store or service the graduate actually uses. If you get cards yourself, here's [how to track gift cards and store credits](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-gift-cards-and-store-credits-without-losing-money/) so they don't go to waste.
- **Practical items:** Bedding, kitchen basics, a good backpack or a work bag. These help most for grads moving out. Ask a parent what's still on the list.
- **Keepsakes:** A framed photo, a letter or a family item. These cost little or nothing and are often remembered longest.

## Setting a graduation budget when you have several graduates

Graduation season can bring a lot of invitations at once. Here's how to keep it manageable:

1. **Add up your graduates first.** List everyone you expect to give to before you buy anything.
2. **Set one season total.** For example (hypothetically), if you can comfortably spend $200 and have four grads, that's about $50 each. You can adjust up or down by relationship.
3. **Tier your list.** Close family gets the larger share, and acquaintances get a card or a smaller amount.
4. **Plan ahead next time.** Graduations are predictable. You can set aside a small amount each month in a [yearly gift fund](https://monee-app.com/blog/birthday-budget-set-a-yearly-gift-fund-without-guessing/) that covers birthdays, graduations and holidays.

Around the same time, you may also be buying end-of-year thank-you gifts. Our guide on [how much to spend on a teacher gift](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-teacher-gift/) can help you budget for those.

## Do you have to bring a gift to a graduation party?

A gift is customary if you attend the party, but it doesn't have to be expensive. If you were only sent an announcement and can't attend, a card with a few heartfelt lines is a polite and fully acceptable response. Families send announcements to share news, not to ask for gifts.

If money is tight, it's fine to give less, or to give your time instead. You could offer to help with a move or review a résumé.

## A note on very large gifts (US)

Most graduation gifts never come near any tax limit, but it helps to know the rules if a relative is planning something big. In the US, the IRS annual gift tax exclusion for 2026 is **$19,000 per recipient**, unchanged from 2025. A married couple can give **$38,000** per recipient ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/frequently-asked-questions-on-gift-taxes)). The IRS also lists tuition you pay for someone as an exclusion separate from the annual one ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/frequently-asked-questions-on-gift-taxes)). There are rules about how the payment has to be made, so check IRS Publication 559 or ask a tax professional before giving a large gift toward school costs. Other countries have different rules.

## Quick recap

- **Typical range:** $25–$100 for most people, and more from close family.
- **Average spending (US):** NRF projected a record $7.2 billion in 2026 graduation spending, and cash was the top gift.
- **Relationship counts most:** the closer you are, the larger or more personal the gift.
- **Match the milestone:** small for early grades, standard for high school and college, personal for advanced degrees.
- **Couples and cookie gifts:** about 1.5 times a single gift for couples, and $15–$40 for a cookie box.
- **Budget for the season:** if you have several grads, set one total and split it.
- **A sincere card always counts:** no graduate will remember the exact dollar amount, but they may remember your words.
