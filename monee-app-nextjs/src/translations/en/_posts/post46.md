**Short answer:** Pay the overdue balance or arrange to pay it. Then call and ask for the late fee to be removed "as a one-time courtesy." Stop talking and let the agent answer. If they say no, ask whether you qualify for a hardship or first-time waiver, or ask for a supervisor. Before you hang up, get the result in writing. Many utilities can waive late charges at their discretion, and some publish a formal one-time waiver policy. Gyms decide case by case, and at chains like Planet Fitness the decision usually rests with your home club.

Late fees aren't a judgment of you. They're a line item set by policy, and the person who answers the phone can often make an exception if you ask clearly.

Principles used throughout:
- Keep it short. One sentence of explanation at most.
- The agent didn't write the policy, so make it easy for them to help you.
- Always get written confirmation before you end the call.

Where amounts or dates appear, fill in the placeholders like [amount] and [date] with your own details.

## Will Planet Fitness waive a late fee?

**Possibly, but there's no guarantee.** Planet Fitness doesn't publish a company-wide late-fee waiver policy that we could find. In the U.S., the fastest route is usually to ask your home club directly, and these points explain why:

- **Most clubs are independently owned.** Planet Fitness says about 90% of its clubs are owned and operated by independent business owners. The system had 2,896 clubs as of December 31, 2025 ([Planet Fitness Q4 & FY2025 results](https://investor.planetfitness.com/investors/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Planet-Fitness-Inc--Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Year-End-2025-Results/default.aspx)). Billing rules and fee decisions can therefore differ from club to club. Planet Fitness's own Australian site also says your local club is the best place for billing issues ([Planet Fitness Australia customer service](https://planetfitnessaustralia.com.au/customer-service/)).
- **The fee amount isn't published in one official place.** Third-party guides often say around $10–$15 per missed payment in the U.S., charged after a failed draft and a retry ([ExercisePick](https://exercisepick.com/how-much-is-planet-fitness-late-fee/)). We couldn't confirm that against an official Planet Fitness source. Check your own membership agreement or ask the club what your agreement says.
- **What helps your case:** the balance is now paid, this is your first late fee, the cause was a bank or card problem that's now fixed, and you're asking soon after the charge.

### How to get a Planet Fitness late fee waived (script)

Call or visit your home club, the location where you signed up. Bring your membership details.

- You: "Hi, I'm a member here, and I see a late fee of [amount] from [date]. The payment issue was [card expired / bank error], and I've updated it and paid the dues. Could you remove the late fee as a one-time courtesy?"
- *(Pause and let them answer.)*
- If no: "I understand. Is there a manager who can approve a one-time courtesy credit? This is my first late fee."
- If yes: "Thank you. Could you note the waiver on my account and email or print a receipt showing the adjusted balance?"
- If they say only the corporate office or billing can do it: "Who exactly should I contact, and could you note on my account that I asked today?"

Before you call, update the payment method on file. That way the next draft won't fail and create a second fee.

## Do utilities waive late fees?

Often, yes, at least once. The rules depend on the provider and, in the U.S., on the state and on whether the utility is municipal or investor-owned.

- **Regulated utilities usually have discretion.** In Pennsylvania (U.S.), for example, state regulations cap utility late charges at 1.5% per month on the overdue portion of the bill. The same rules say "a public utility may waive late payment charges on any customer accounts" ([52 Pa. Code § 56.22](https://pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/052/chapter56/s56.22.html)). Those rules also bar late charges on disputed estimated bills in most cases.
- **Many city utilities publish a one-time waiver policy, usually with conditions.** Three U.S. examples:
  - **Berea, Kentucky:** allows a one-time waiver for extenuating circumstances if you have 12+ months of payment history and ask within 30 days ([Berea Code § 31.105](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bereaky/latest/berea_ky/0-0-0-35482)).
  - **West University Place, Texas:** waives a late charge on a written request within 30 days if you haven't had one in the past 24 months ([West University Place request form](https://westutx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3015/Request-for-Late-Fee-Waiver)).
  - **Gardner, Kansas:** offers a one-time waiver on a written request within 30 days of the delinquent notice ([City of Gardner policy](https://gardnerkansas.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/11798/638258097788370000)).
- **Takeaway:** ask quickly, mention your on-time history, and ask whether a written form exists.

If cost is the real issue rather than a one-off slip, U.S. households can apply for the federal Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) through their state office or local community action agency. It can help with heating and cooling bills and with disconnection emergencies ([NCOA](https://ncoa.org/article/what-is-the-low-income-home-energy-assistance-program-liheap)). Ask your utility about payment plans too.

## Call map (one screen)

- **Open:** "Hi, I'm calling about a late fee on my account."
- **Ask:** "Please waive the late fee as a courtesy."
- **Pause:** Stay quiet and let the agent respond.
- **Counter:** "Please check if I'm eligible for a one-time or hardship waiver."
- **Escalate:** "Could a supervisor review this for a one-time exception?"
- **Confirm:** "Please send the confirmation to [email]."
- **Close:** "Thanks for your help today."

## Utility company: role-play

- Caller: "Hi, I'm calling about a late fee on my account ending in [last 4 digits]. I paid on [date]. I'd like to request that the late fee be waived as a courtesy."
- Agent: "We apply late fees automatically. I don't think we can remove it."
- Caller: "I understand it's automatic. Please check if I'm eligible for a one-time or hardship waiver today."
- Agent: "Let me check."
- Caller: "Thank you. I'll hold."
- Agent: "We can remove [amount] this time."
- Caller: "I appreciate that. Please confirm the waiver and my current balance by email to [email]."
- Caller: "Before we finish, can you confirm my due date and whether autopay, due-date changes or alerts are available?"

**If pushback continues:**
- Caller: "Understood. I've been a customer since [year] with on-time payments apart from this. Can you escalate to a supervisor for a one-time courtesy?"

**Supervisor:**
- Caller: "I paid on [date], and I'm asking for a one-time courtesy waiver. If a full waiver isn't possible, please check for a partial credit, a written waiver form, or a hardship or payment-assistance program."

**If the answer is still no:**
- Caller: "I appreciate the review. Please note my request on the account. Thank you."

You can call back later and ask again. A different agent may decide differently.

## Gym: role-play (any chain)

- Caller: "Hi, I'm calling about a late fee on my membership ending in [last 4 digits]. I paid on [date]. I'd like the late fee waived as a courtesy."
- Agent: "We don't waive late fees."
- Caller: "I hear you. Please check if there's a one-time courtesy credit, a medical or hardship exception, or a loyalty waiver."
- Agent: "We can't waive it, but we can apply a credit toward next month."
- Caller: "A credit works. Please confirm by email the fee amount, the credit applied, and my next billing date."

**If they upsell instead:**
- Agent: "If you upgrade, we can waive it."
- Caller: "I'm not upgrading to resolve a late fee. I'm asking for a one-time courtesy waiver or a credit. If that's not possible, please transfer me to a manager."

**Manager:**
- Caller: "I paid on [date]. I'm asking for a one-time courtesy waiver of [amount] or a credit, with written confirmation."

**If you're genuinely ready to cancel:**
- Caller: "If a waiver or credit isn't possible, I'd like to discuss canceling. Are any fees waived as part of that?"
- If they offer a retention courtesy: "If I keep the membership, can you apply that waiver now and confirm by email?"
- If you cancel: "Please confirm in writing any waivers, my end date [date], and any remaining balance."

Only bring up cancellation if you mean it. And check your contract first, because it may include notice periods, a buyout, or an annual fee.

**A note on U.S. cancellation rules:** In 2024 the FTC finalized a federal "click-to-cancel" rule. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated it on July 8, 2025, before full enforcement began ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated-ahead-of-planned-july-14-effective-date)). Federal law such as ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws, such as California's, still apply. How you can cancel therefore depends on your state and your contract.

## Short scripts by scenario

1. **First late fee:** "I paid on [date] and saw a late fee of [amount]. Would you remove it as a one-time courtesy and email confirmation to [email]?"
2. **Autopay or bank failure:** "My payment method failed on [date]. It's fixed and paid. Please waive the late fee as a courtesy."
3. **Hardship, medical or caregiving:** "I had [brief description] around [date], which affected payment timing. Please apply a hardship or courtesy waiver." If they ask for proof: "Which document do you need, and where should I send it?"
4. **Several late fees:** "I've paid the balance. I'm requesting a courtesy review of [amount] and any eligible fees." Fallback: "If full removal isn't possible, please check for a partial credit."
5. **Disputed or estimated bill (utilities):** "This bill was estimated, and I'm disputing the reading. Please hold or remove the late charge while the dispute is reviewed."

## Handling common curveballs

- **"It's company policy."** "I understand. I'm requesting a one-time exception. If you can't approve it, please escalate to someone who reviews exceptions."
- **"You've had a waiver before."** "Thanks for checking. Given my payment on [date], is a partial credit or hardship review possible?"
- **"We can't email."** "Please add a note to my account summarizing the waiver and read it back to me, plus a reference number."
- **"The system won't let me."** "Could a supervisor or the billing team check? They sometimes have different adjustment tools."
- **"Do you want autopay?"** "I'll look at that. Today I'm confirming the waiver and the adjusted balance."

## Pacing the call

- Open with one sentence, then pause. Silence gives the agent room to offer something.
- Repeat your request once, then escalate. Asking for a supervisor is a standard, respectful request.
- Keep your voice level. When they speed up, slow down.

## FAQ

**Can I get a Planet Fitness late fee waived?**
You can ask. Contact your home club, explain the cause in one sentence, show that the balance is paid, and ask for a one-time courtesy. Most clubs are franchise-owned, so the answer depends on the club.

**Do I need to explain why I was late?**
Briefly. One sentence is enough.

**Should I apologize?**
A short "thanks for your help" works better than a long apology.

**What if they tell me to call back?**
Ask for a reference number or an account note, then call again.

**Can I get several fees waived?**
Sometimes. Ask for a "courtesy review of any eligible fees" and accept partial wins.

**How do I stop it happening again?**
Line up your due dates with payday, set up autopay, and keep a small buffer. The same system works for any bill: see [how to avoid late fees with a due-date, autopay and buffer plan](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-avoid-credit-card-late-fees-with-a-duedate-autopay-buffer-plan/).
