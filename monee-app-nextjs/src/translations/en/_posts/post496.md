Yes, buying clothes off-season is often a good idea, but only when you're confident you'll wear the item next year. A low clearance price doesn't help if the coat sits in a closet, no longer fits, or feels dated by the time the weather turns. The discount is only worth something if the item gets worn.

Let's make this simpler. Below is a short test you can run on any off-season item, in the store or in your online cart.

## Why off-season prices drop in the first place

Here's how it works. Retailers need room for the next season's stock, so they mark down what's left as a season ends. Consumer finance guides, like this one from [a US credit union](https://www.climbcu.org/explore/learn/empowered-living-blog/empowered-living-blog/2025/02/18/best-times-to-buy-clothing), say the best time to buy clothes for a season is when that season is on its way out.

So the timing works in your favor. What it can't do is fill the gap between buying and wearing. If you buy a swimsuit in September, you might not wear it for eight or nine months, and a lot can change in that time.

## The hidden cost of "a deal I never wore"

Picture this: two winter coats.

- **Coat A** (hypothetical): $180 at full price in November. You wear it 60 times over the winter.
- **Coat B** (hypothetical): $90 on clearance in February. You wear it 5 times, because by next winter it feels too tight and you reach for an older coat instead.

Coat A works out to $3 per wear. Coat B works out to $18 per wear. The cheaper coat ended up costing much more per wear. This is the same logic behind a [cost-per-wear test for mending clothes](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-mend-clothes-a-cost-per-wear-test/): the price tag matters less than how often you use the item.

There's a wider cost too. In the US, the [Environmental Protection Agency estimates](https://www.epa.gov/facts-and-figures-about-materials-waste-and-recycling/textiles-material-specific-data) that about 17 million tons of textiles were generated as municipal waste in 2018, mostly from discarded clothing, and that landfills received 11.3 million tons of them. Unworn bargains add to that.

## The Wear-Next-Year Test

Ask yourself these five questions before you buy. If the item passes at least four, it's probably a real deal. If it fails two or more, skip it.

### 1. Does it replace something, or just add something?

Off-season buys work best when they fill a gap you already know about, like boots that leak, a jacket with a broken zipper, or a child's coat they've clearly outgrown. If you can name the item it replaces, that's a good sign. If you're thinking "it'd be nice to have," pause.

### 2. Will it still fit in 6–12 months?

This question matters most for:

- **Kids' clothes.** Children grow quickly. If you buy ahead, buy one size up, and only for basics like coats, snow pants, or swimwear.
- **Clothes for a body that's changing.** Pregnancy, training, recovery, or medication changes can all affect fit. Stick to forgiving cuts or wait.
- **Shoes.** Feet can change too, and shoes are hard to alter.

### 3. Is it a classic or a trend?

Basics hold up from year to year: plain wool sweaters, a simple dark coat, solid swim shorts, good-quality socks. Trend pieces, like a very specific color, cut, or print, are riskier because they can feel dated before you wear them. The thinking here is close to the [feature-gap test for last year's model](https://monee-app.com/blog/is-last-years-model-worth-buying-a-feature-gap-test/): when the newer version barely differs from the old one, the discount is pure savings.

### 4. Can you return it?

Clearance items often come with shorter return windows, and sometimes they're final sale. Check the store's policy before you pay. If you can't return it, the other four answers need to be a clear yes, especially on fit.

### 5. Would you pay this price for it in-season?

Imagine it's the first cold week of next winter and you see this exact item at this exact price. Would you buy it then? If yes, buying now just locks in a lower price. If you'd walk past it, the discount is doing the deciding for you.

## A quick scoring guide

| Test result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 5 out of 5 | Clear need, fits, timeless, low risk | Buy it |
| 4 out of 5 | Strong case with one small risk | Buy it if the weak point is minor (e.g., final sale but you're sure of the fit) |
| 3 out of 5 | It could go either way | Wait a day, or put it in your cart and come back later |
| 2 or fewer | Bargain appeal, little real need | Skip it |

## Which items usually pass, and which usually don't

**Often good off-season buys:**

- Winter coats, boots, and thermal layers bought late in winter
- Swimwear and sandals bought at the end of summer, if your size is stable
- Basics you replace regularly, like socks, underwear, and plain T-shirts
- Kids' outerwear one size up

**Often poor off-season buys:**

- Trend-driven pieces
- Event outfits for an occasion you haven't planned yet (for one-off events, [renting an outfit](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-rent-an-outfit-a-cost-per-wear-test/) may be cheaper per wear)
- Anything you'd describe as "such a good deal" without naming when you'd wear it
- Final-sale shoes you haven't tried on

## How to make off-season buying fit your budget

Off-season deals can upset a monthly budget, because you're spending now on something you'll use later. A few habits help:

- **Keep a short "next season" list.** Write down real gaps when you notice them, like a hole in your gloves in January. Shop from that list when the sales come.
- **Set a seasonal clothing cap.** Choose an amount in advance for each end-of-season sale and stay under it.
- **Track what you actually wear.** If last year's off-season buys never got worn, that tells you to buy less ahead next time.
- **Fit it into bigger goals.** If you're cutting back overall, off-season buying can fit into a [low-buy year you'll actually stick with](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-make-a-lowbuy-year-youll-actually-stick-with/), as long as the item is on your planned list.

Buying clothes off-season is a timing decision, and it's a lot like deciding whether to [buy gifts early or wait](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-buy-gifts-early-or-wait-a-budget-timing-test/). Buying early saves money when the need is certain and costs money when it isn't. Run the Wear-Next-Year Test, and the discount only counts when you'll actually wear what you bought.
