The best way to catch grocery checkout errors is to check at three points: when you choose an item, while it scans, and before you leave. Focus on prices, quantities, and discounts rather than trying to judge whether the final total “looks right.”

A mismatch deserves a check, but it does not automatically mean someone acted dishonestly. The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology notes that pricing errors can include both overcharges and undercharges. [NIST’s price verification guidance](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/faqs/price-verification-faqs) explains how officials compare displayed prices with checkout charges.

## Check the shelf label before checkout

For anything you choose because of a special offer, read the whole label. Match the product name, package size, and variety to the item in your basket.

Look for conditions such as:

- A loyalty membership requirement.
- A digital coupon you must activate.
- A minimum purchase quantity.
- A limit on discounted items.
- The offer’s start and end dates.

Photograph a label if the offer is complicated or the price seems unclear. Include the product description and conditions, so the photo provides more context than a large price alone.

Keep package prices and comparison prices separate when checking. Look carefully at whether a figure refers to the whole pack, one item, or a unit of weight.

## Watch the items and quantities as they scan

At a staffed checkout, position yourself where you can see the customer display. At self-checkout, check the entry after each scan, especially if you are unsure whether an item registered.

Watch for:

| What to check | What to compare |
|---|---|
| Item charged twice | The number charged against the number in your basket |
| Unexpected price | The scanned price against the matching shelf label |
| Wrong produce selection | The displayed name against the variety you selected |
| Quantity mismatch | Individual items, multipacks, and any quantity entered |
| Missing markdown | The charge against the reduced-price sticker |

For loose produce, check the product selection and price per unit of weight. Keep the item fully on the scale without resting a hand or another bag on it. If the weight looks questionable, ask for a reweigh.

Raise a possible mistake when you notice it. A simple phrase keeps the discussion specific: “Could you check that entry? There’s one in the basket, but the screen shows two.”

## Confirm discounts before paying

Before you approve payment, ask whether all loyalty discounts and coupons have been applied. Check the resulting amount for the items you expected to be discounted.

Follow your own retailer’s instructions. For example, U.S. retailer Kroger says customers redeem digital coupons by adding the offer, buying qualifying items, and scanning their Shopper’s Card or entering their alternate ID. Its receipts identify digital coupon deductions beneath qualifying items with “ecpn.” [Kroger’s digital coupon FAQs](https://www.kroger.com/hc/help/faqs/ways-to-save/coupons) describe the process.

If a discount is missing, check the offer’s conditions before assuming a scanner error. Confirm the size, variety, quantity, expiry date, and account used.

**Hypothetical example:** You expect two qualifying items priced at $3 each to receive a $1 combined discount. Their combined cost should be $5 before any applicable tax. If the receipt lists $6 for the items and a separate $1 deduction, the discount is present. Without that deduction, the offer needs checking.

## Review the receipt before leaving

Move to a spot where you can read the receipt without blocking the checkout. For a digital receipt, open the itemized version if it is available.

Start with the items whose prices you noted, then check:

- **Repeated entries:** Do they match the quantities purchased?
- **Offers and markdowns:** Is each expected reduction included?
- **Removed items:** Has the charge been reversed?
- **Unfamiliar descriptions:** Can you match them to something in your bags?

Read related charges and deductions together. A correction is complete only when the final amount reflects it; an original charge may still be visible alongside its reversal.

If time is tight, prioritize expensive items and purchases you made specifically because of a discount. That gives your review a clear focus without requiring you to memorize every shelf price.

## Resolve a mismatch with specific evidence

Show the cashier or customer service desk the receipt entry, the product, and any matching label photo. You can say: “This item shows a different price from the shelf label. Could you check the price and offer conditions?”

Before payment, ask for the entry to be corrected. After payment, ask staff to review the overcharge and explain the adjustment. Keep any correction or refund receipt with the original.

Do not assume a pricing mistake automatically means a free item or a fixed compensation payment. In the United States, pricing rules vary by state; NIST directs retail overcharge complaints to the relevant state weights and measures authority. [NIST’s guidance on pricing laws and complaints](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/faqs/price-verification-faqs) provides the appropriate starting point.

A useful receipt check comes down to three matches: the products you took, the quantities you bought, and the prices and discounts that apply. The final total alone cannot show you all three.
