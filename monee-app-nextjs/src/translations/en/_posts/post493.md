To stop duplicate gifts, the whole family needs **one shared list where givers can claim an item before they buy it.** The person getting the gifts shouldn't be able to see those claims. Everyone else should.

That's really all there is to it. A list on its own won't stop duplicates. The claiming step is what does.

Below you'll find how to set one up, how to keep the surprise, and how to handle the relatives who'd rather shop without an app.

## Why duplicate gifts happen

Duplicate gifts usually come from good intentions. Two aunts hear the same hint. A grandparent and a parent both spot the same toy on sale.

Nobody did anything wrong. They just couldn't see what the others were planning.

A family wish list gives everyone that view, without spoiling things for the person opening the gifts.

## Step 1: Pick one place for the list

The tool you pick matters less than everyone using the same one. Two lists in two places can bring the duplicate problem right back.

Common options:

- **A dedicated wish list app.** On Giftster, for example, list owners [can't see which items have been reserved or purchased, but other shoppers can](https://help.giftster.com/article/72-will-list-maker-see-reserved-purchased). Elfster works in a similar way: the owner [isn't notified when a wish is marked as purchased](https://help.elfster.com/knowledge/how-do-i-mark-an-item-as-purchased-on-someones-wish-list).
- **A retailer's gift list.** Amazon's Gift List has a ["Keep it a Surprise" setting](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G85CQ7LT2BVSPWWE) that hides purchased items from the owner. Shoppers still see what has already been bought.
- **A shared spreadsheet or document.** It's free and flexible. The catch is that you have to keep the gift recipient off the shared copy yourself.

If your family shops at lots of different stores, a store-neutral app or spreadsheet is usually the easiest choice. Giftster, for one, [doesn't sell anything](https://help.giftster.com/article/69-mark-gift-purchased), so you can buy the item wherever you like.

## Step 2: Ask each person to write a useful list

A vague list leads to guessing, and guessing leads to doubles.

Ask everyone to include:

- **Specifics.** Size, colour, model, or a link.
- **A rough price range.** Givers can then pick something that fits their budget.
- **More items than they need.** A few extra ideas mean nobody gets stuck.
- **"Already have" notes**, if useful. For example: "Have the first two books in this series."

You might also add a few non-physical ideas, like experiences or a contribution toward something bigger. If cost is a sensitive subject in your family, here's how to [ask relatives to keep gifts budget-friendly](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-ask-relatives-to-keep-gifts-budget-friendly/) without it feeling awkward.

## Step 3: Make the claiming rule clear

This is the step that actually stops duplicates. Agree on one simple rule:

> **Claim it before you buy it. Mark it purchased right after.**

In Giftster, for example, you [reserve an item first and mark it purchased as a separate step](https://help.giftster.com/article/69-mark-gift-purchased). Once it's marked purchased, other shoppers see it as unavailable.

Encourage people to claim early, even if they won't buy for a while. Holding a claim costs nothing, and it stops someone else from grabbing the same idea. If you're unsure when to actually buy, this [budget timing test for buying gifts early or waiting](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-buy-gifts-early-or-wait-a-budget-timing-test/) can help.

One caution with store gift lists: buying the same item outside the list may not update it. If you shop somewhere else, go back and mark the item yourself.

## Step 4: Protect the surprise

You want givers to see claims. You don't want the recipient to see them.

A few habits help:

- **Check the visibility settings** before you share the list. Defaults vary between tools.
- **Watch out for kids' lists.** On Giftster, parents who manage a child's list [can see the reserved and purchased status](https://help.giftster.com/article/72-will-list-maker-see-reserved-purchased). That's handy for parents, but it's worth knowing.
- **Keep spreadsheets private.** Make a separate "shopping copy" that the recipient isn't invited to.

## Step 5: Include the relatives who don't use apps

Some family members won't download anything, and that's fine.

Choose one person to act as the go-between. That relative calls or texts them, then claims items on their behalf.

Some tools also let guests mark purchases without signing up. On Giftster's public lists, for example, [non-members can mark items purchased](https://help.giftster.com/article/72-will-list-maker-see-reserved-purchased). Even so, one point person is often simpler.

## Step 6: Handle shared and group gifts

Bigger items sometimes get split between several people. To keep it tidy:

- Add a note on the item, like "Group gift: Mum, Sam, and Lee."
- Have one person claim it for the whole group.
- Settle up soon after buying, so nobody quietly covers too much.

Splitting money between relatives can get tricky. A [simple budget-first rule for lending money to family](https://monee-app.com/blog/lending-money-to-family-a-simple-budget-first-rule/) can help you think it through.

## A quick hypothetical example

Imagine a family of eight shopping for two kids. Everyone uses one shared app. Each child's list has about ten items, and each one includes a price range.

Grandma claims a board game in October. Two weeks later, an uncle opens the list, sees the game is taken, and picks a book set instead.

There was no group chat back-and-forth and no awkward return afterwards. It worked because claiming came before buying.

## Small habits that keep it working

- **Update the list as wishes change.** Remove items the person has already bought for themselves.
- **Set one "list ready by" date** so early shoppers aren't choosing from a half-finished list.
- **Reuse the list for birthdays.** The same setup works all year.
- **Be gentle with slip-ups.** Someone will forget to mark a purchase at some point. A friendly reminder works better than a rule lecture.

## The short version

One shared list. Clear, specific wishes. Claim before you buy, and mark it purchased right after. Keep claims hidden from the person receiving the gifts.

Once those few habits are in place, a family wish list does most of the coordinating for you, and you can stop wondering whether someone else already bought that gift.
