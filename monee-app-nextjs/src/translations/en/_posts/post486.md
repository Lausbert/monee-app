Somebody tears open the envelope, reads a name and freezes. It's the coworker they've spoken to twice, both times about the printer. Then comes the real question: *how much is this supposed to cost?*

**The short answer: most Secret Santa gifts cost between $10 and $30.** Elfster, a popular gift-exchange platform, says [spending limits usually fall in that range](https://www.elfster.com/blog/2023/12/03/a-guide-to-secret-santa-rules-for-beginners/), depending on the group. If your group already set a limit, spend that amount. If nobody set one, aim for about $20 to $25 at work, and adjust for friends and family as explained below.

## Why the number matters more than the gift

Secret Santa only works if every gift feels roughly equal. If one person spends $8 and another spends $60, the gift stops being fun and starts being awkward. The person who overspent feels silly. The person who underspent feels worse.

A clear limit keeps things fair, so the fun can come from how clever the gift is, not how much it cost.

Elfster also suggests using [one fixed number instead of a range](https://www.elfster.com/blog/family-secret-santa), because "around $30" quietly turns into $20 for some people and $50 for others. "$25" is clear. "$20–$40" just starts an argument.

## Typical Secret Santa budgets by group

These are common starting points, not rules. Your group's budget always wins.

| Group | Typical budget (US) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Office or workplace | $15–$25 | Easy for everyone at different pay levels |
| Friends | $20–$40 | A little more personal, still low-pressure |
| Extended family | About $25 | Elfster's [suggestion for extended family](https://www.elfster.com/blog/family-secret-santa) |
| Close family | $50–$75 | Elfster's [suggestion for closer relatives](https://www.elfster.com/blog/family-secret-santa) |
| School or kids' groups | $10–$15 | Keeps it fair for parents |

For readers outside the US, here's a point of comparison: a 2021 Mastercard survey found that people in the UK spent an average of [£12.16 on a Secret Santa gift](https://www.mastercard.com/news/europe/en-uk/newsroom/press-releases/en-gb/2021/november/what-do-brits-really-think-of-secret-santa). That number is a few years old, so treat it as a rough guide, not today's price.

## Where Secret Santa fits in your holiday budget

A $25 gift feels small until you add up everything else the season costs. In the US, the National Retail Federation found that people planned to spend [$890.49 each on the 2025 holidays](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-to-spend-second-highest-amount-on-record-according-to-nrf-holiday-survey), and $627.93 of that was for gifts. For 2026, NRF chose not to publish a per-person figure. Instead, it [forecasts total November–December retail sales growing 4.2%](https://nrf.com/research-insights/holiday-data-and-trends/winter-holidays/winter-holiday-faqs), and notes that shoppers are watching prices closely.

So your Secret Santa gift is one line in a much bigger budget. If you're in three gift exchanges, that $25 is really $75. Count them all before December starts. If you already keep a [yearly gift fund for birthdays](https://monee-app.com/blog/birthday-budget-set-a-yearly-gift-fund-without-guessing/), add gift exchanges to it as their own category.

## How to pick the right amount if you're the organizer

Getting to organize means getting to set the number. Here's a simple way to do it:

1. **Start with the person who has the least to spend.** At work, that might be an intern or a part-time worker. The limit should feel easy for them.
2. **Pick one number.** "$20" is better than "$15–$25."
3. **Say whether shipping, gift wrap and cards count toward the limit.** Most groups leave them out, but say so either way.
4. **Make it anonymous to opt out.** Some people would rather sit this one out for money reasons. A private sign-up form lets them skip without explaining.
5. **Announce it early.** People shop better and cheaper with three weeks' notice than with three days'.

## How to stay at the limit without the gift feeling cheap

Being careful with money and being thoughtful can go together. A few ideas that help:

- **Spend the limit, not more.** Going over can make other people feel bad about their gifts. Going a dollar or two under is fine.
- **Specific beats expensive.** A $15 hot sauce sampler for someone who puts sriracha on everything will go over better than a $30 generic candle.
- **Gift cards are a safe pick.** NRF found gift cards were the [most-wanted holiday gift in the US in 2025](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-to-spend-second-highest-amount-on-record-according-to-nrf-holiday-survey), named by 50% of shoppers. If you're getting one back, here's how to [keep track of gift cards so they don't go to waste](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-gift-cards-and-store-credits-without-losing-money/).
- **Keep wrapping simple.** A $20 gift in $8 of wrapping paper is really a $28 gift. These tips on [cutting gift wrap and card costs](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-cut-gift-wrap-and-greeting-card-costs/) help the budget go further.
- **Look at the wish list.** Many Secret Santa apps let people list what they like. Reading it isn't cheating. It's the point.

## A quick hypothetical

Take a hypothetical office group of 12 with a $20 limit. One person spends $19 on a nice notebook and a pen in the recipient's favorite color. Another spends $20 on a gift card to the coffee shop downstairs. A third goes over and spends $45 on a Bluetooth speaker.

The speaker gets a polite "wow," and then everyone quietly looks at their own $20 gift. The notebook and the gift card get real thanks. Same party, same budget, and very different moods.

## What if you can't afford the limit?

Then say so, early and privately, to the organizer. Asking for a lower limit or stepping out isn't rude. It's just good with money. Some groups switch to a homemade or regift-only exchange, which can be more fun anyway. And if December feels tight overall, a [no-spend weekend](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-run-a-no-spend-weekend-without-feeling-deprived/) can give your budget some room.

## The bottom line

For most Secret Santa exchanges, **$20 to $25 is the sweet spot**. It's enough for something thoughtful and low enough that nobody worries about it. Work groups can go lower, close families can go higher, and the group's agreed number always comes first. Pick one figure, stick to it, and spend your effort on choosing the gift, not on how much it costs.

The printer coworker? They'll probably love a good coffee mug. Most people do.
