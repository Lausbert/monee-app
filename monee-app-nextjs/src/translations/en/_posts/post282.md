**Short answer:** If an item's price drops after you buy it, contact the same retailer within its adjustment window. Give your order number or receipt, the date you bought it, the price you paid and proof of the current lower price. Then ask them to refund the difference. Several large US retailers have a formal policy for this. Costco gives 30 days, Target gives 14 days and Best Buy uses your return period. Others, such as Walmart.com and most of Amazon, don't promise adjustments. Even then, a polite request or a return and rebuy can still get you the lower price.

You don't need to be pushy. You need a clear reason, a short script and a calm way to say it. A price adjustment is an ordinary customer service request, not a favor and not a confrontation.

**Simple script**

"I bought this on [date], and I noticed the price has changed since then. I'm wondering if you're able to honor the current price and refund the difference. I'm happy to share my order details if that helps."

That's enough to start.

## What information you need for a price adjustment request

Whether you noticed the drop yourself or got a price-drop alert from an app, browser extension or the retailer, the request usually needs the same details. Gather them before you contact the store:

- **Order number or receipt.** A digital receipt, paper receipt or packing slip all work.
- **Purchase date.** Adjustment windows usually count from this date. Best Buy is an exception and counts from the day you receive the item (see below).
- **Exact item.** Give the name plus the item number, SKU or model number. Most policies require an identical product: same brand, size, color and model.
- **Price you paid and current price.** Include the quantity if you bought more than one.
- **Where the lower price appears.** Say whether it's the website, the app or a store, and have the product link ready.
- **Proof of the lower price.** A screenshot or the price-drop alert email helps with online requests. Some stores won't accept screenshots. Target, for example, requires the live price on your phone, checked by a team member ([Target](https://www.target.com/help/articles/policies-guidelines/price-match-guarantee)).
- **The posted policy.** Note the retailer's adjustment window and exclusions so you know what you're asking for.

A price-drop alert tells you to act. It doesn't count as a request. You still have to contact the retailer or use its request form. Knowing your numbers makes the conversation easier: you're not calling with a vague feeling, you're pointing to a specific change.

## Price adjustment policies at major US retailers (October 2026)

These are US policies, and retailers change them often. Check the linked page before you ask.

| Retailer (US) | Adjusts its own price drops after purchase? | Window | Key limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Costco | Yes | 30 days from purchase | Same channel only. No competitor matching |
| Target | Yes | 14 days from purchase | Target prices only. No screenshots |
| Best Buy | Yes | During your return/exchange period (15 days for most standard customers) | Excludes open-box, clearance, refurbished, Marketplace |
| Walmart.com | No formal policy | — | Return and rebuy, or cancel and reorder |
| Amazon.com | No general policy | — | Pre-order Price Guarantee on eligible items |

### Costco: price reduced after purchase

Costco says purchases that drop in price within 30 days of the purchase date can get a price adjustment ([Costco Customer Service](https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/628)). The details that matter:

- **Online orders:** Use the "Request a price match" option on Costco.com. Approved credits usually reach your original payment method in 5–10 business days.
- **Warehouse purchases:** Go to the returns counter at the warehouse where you bought the item.
- **Same channel only:** Costco won't adjust an online purchase to a lower warehouse price, because online prices include shipping and handling.
- **Promotions with set dates:** You must ask during the promotion *and* within 30 days of purchase. Limit-per-member quantities still apply.
- **Exclusions:** Costco doesn't match other retailers, and precious metals aren't eligible. Costco can also deny a request at its discretion.

### Target

Target matches its own lower prices at checkout or within 14 days after purchase. That covers Target.com, Target stores and automatically applied Target Circle deals. Since July 28, 2025, it no longer matches competitors such as Amazon and Walmart ([Target](https://www.target.com/help/articles/policies-guidelines/price-match-guarantee); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/target-price-matching-policy-july-2025)). To ask:

- **Store purchases:** Bring your receipt and the live lower price to Guest Services.
- **Online orders:** Use chat or call 1‑800‑591‑3869 with your order number ready.
- **Exclusions:** Clearance, open-box and refurbished items, typo prices and a lower price at a different Target store don't qualify.

### Best Buy

Best Buy's Price Match Guarantee covers its own price drops during your return and exchange period. That's 15 days for most products for standard customers, and the clock starts when you receive the item. Paid My Best Buy members get longer windows ([Priceva](https://priceva.com/blog/best-buy-price-match); [Visualping](https://visualping.io/pages/blog/best-buy-price-match)). Third-party summaries disagree on the exact member window, so check it in your account before you rely on it. Open-box, clearance, refurbished and Marketplace items are excluded. Only the original buyer can ask.

### Walmart

Reports on Walmart's US price match policy say Walmart.com doesn't adjust prices on items you've already bought. US stores will match the Walmart.com price of an identical in-stock item at checkout ([ShopBack](https://www.shopback.com/guide/shopping/still-money-back-after-checkout-if-forgot-use); [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/01/17/what-is-price-adjustment-and-how-can-you-take-advantage-of-it/)). If you've already paid, your realistic options are:

- **Not shipped yet:** Cancel and reorder.
- **Already received:** Return and rebuy within the return window.

### Amazon

Amazon.com has no general post-purchase price adjustment policy ([DealNews](https://dealnews.com/features/amazon/price-adjustment/)). The main exception is the Pre-order Price Guarantee. On eligible pre-orders that show the guarantee message, you pay the lowest Amazon.com price offered between your order and the end of release day ([HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/amazon-gta-6-preorder-price-guarantee-secure-your-copy)). For anything else, you can ask customer service for a one-time courtesy credit. If that fails, return and rebuy within the return window.

### Credit card price protection

Some credit cards still refund a price drop even when the store won't. Fewer cards offer this than a few years ago, because several issuers have cut or removed it ([Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/which-credit-cards-offer-price-protection/)). In the US, Capital One currently has the most cards that still include it ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/credit-card-price-protection-policies/)). Check your card's current benefits guide for the claim window, per-claim cap and excluded items.

## Email script

**Subject line:** Price adjustment request for order [order number]

Hello,

I placed an order on [date] for [item name, item/SKU number] at [price paid]. The current price on your site is [current price], and I'm still within your [X]-day adjustment window.

Could you adjust the price and refund the difference of [amount]? My order number is [order number], and I've attached a screenshot of the current price.

If you need anything else from me, I'm happy to provide it. Thank you for taking a look.

Best,
[Your name]

## Chat script

"Hi, I bought [item name] on [date], and I noticed the price has dropped since then. Can you help me with a price adjustment for my order?"

If they ask for details:

"Sure, my order number is [order number]. I paid [price], and it's now [current price]. Here's the product link: [link]."

## Phone script

"Hi, I'm calling about a recent purchase. I bought [item name] on [date], and I noticed the price is now lower. I wanted to ask if you can honor the new price and refund the difference."

Then pause and let them respond.

If you tend to get flustered on calls, keep this note in front of you:

- I bought it on [date] for [price]
- It's now [current price]
- I'm asking for the difference to be refunded
- I have my order number ready

## If they say no

This is where most people give up too early. A first no isn't always a final no. Sometimes you just need a different angle.

**If they say:** "We don't do price adjustments."
**You say:** "Thanks for letting me know. Since this was a very recent purchase, is there anything else you can offer in this situation?"

That keeps the conversation open. You might not get a direct adjustment, but you could be offered store credit, a one-time exception or a return-and-rebuy option.

**If they say:** "The promotion doesn't apply to past purchases."
**You say:** "I understand. Since I purchased so recently, would you be willing to make a one-time exception?"

**If they say:** "You'll need to return the item and reorder."
**You say:** "If that's the only option, I can consider it. Before I do that, can you confirm whether there's a simpler way to adjust the order?"

**If they say:** "It's outside the adjustment window."
**You say:** "Understood. I'd still appreciate it if you could check whether any exception is possible."

You're not arguing. You're asking one more time, clearly and calmly. If what you want is money back for a problem with the item rather than a price change, see [how to ask for a partial refund without awkwardness](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-partial-refund-without-awkwardness/).

## If the first try doesn't work

Try one of these next steps:

- **Switch channels.** Chat, phone and email agents sometimes have different flexibility.
- **Ask politely for a supervisor** or a senior agent.
- **Check the return window.** Returning and rebuying is often the practical fallback when there's no adjustment policy. Make sure the new price is still live and the item is in stock first.
- **Check your credit card's price protection** if the store won't help.
- **Keep your message shorter.** A long explanation can weaken a simple request.

Here's a follow-up email you can use:

Hello,

I'm following up on my earlier message about my order from [date]. I understand the standard policy may not allow a price adjustment, but since the price changed so soon after my purchase, I wanted to ask once more whether any exception is possible.

I'd appreciate your help and am happy to provide any additional details.

Best,
[Your name]

## Is it worth the time?

For a few dollars, maybe not. For a big-ticket item bought just before a sale, often yes. To decide quickly, use this [time-to-save test for price matching](https://monee-app.com/blog/is-price-matching-worth-it-a-timetosave-test/). To catch price drops without checking every day, set an alert for anything expensive you've bought recently. For groceries, a simple [grocery price book](https://monee-app.com/blog/build-a-grocery-price-book-a-20minute-savemore-template/) helps you notice a real sale.

## A few things that help

- **Use a steady tone.** Not apologetic, not aggressive. You're making a reasonable request.
- **Keep it brief, and lead with the ask,** not a long story.
- **Ask early.** Windows are short, and some stores also require the lower price to still be live and the item in stock.

If you feel nervous, open with this line:

"I'm not sure what your policy is, but I wanted to ask whether a price adjustment is possible."

That sentence works because it's respectful, direct and easy to answer.

The goal isn't to sound perfect. The goal is to ask. A calm, factual request often gets better results than people expect. Even if the answer is no, you'll have practiced something useful: saying what you need clearly, with your numbers in front of you.
