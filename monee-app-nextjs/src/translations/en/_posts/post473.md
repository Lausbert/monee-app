For most people, a housewarming gift costs between **$25 and $75**, and around **$40** is common. Where you land in that range depends on how close you are to the host. A small, thoughtful gift is fine. You don't need to spend a lot to show you care.

Here's how to choose an amount that fits your relationship and your budget, so you can enjoy the party without second-guessing what you brought.

## What do people actually spend?

Ally, a US bank, [asked 1,000 people about their gifting habits](https://ally.com/stories/spend/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-gift-for-any-occasion) in 2023. For housewarmings, the typical (median) spend was **$40**. People who spent more than most gave about **$75**.

Spending also changed with the relationship:

| Relationship to host | Typical housewarming spend (Ally survey, USD) |
|---|---|
| Acquaintance, coworker or neighbor | $25 |
| Close friend | $50 |
| Family member | $75 |

The survey figures are medians rounded up to the nearest $5, and the article doesn't say much about how the survey was run. Treat them as a reasonable reference point, not a rule. If you live outside the US, scale the idea to local prices. A modest, practical gift is the norm almost everywhere.

## Etiquette: is a gift even required?

Not strictly. The Emily Post Institute says the point of a housewarming is to [warm the home with the presence of friends and neighbors](https://emilypost.substack.com/p/etiquette-today-all-about-hosting). Gifts are lovely but not required, and hosts shouldn't set up a registry.

In practice, many guests still like to bring something small. If money is tight, a modest gift, or even some homemade food, is perfectly polite. If the invitation says "no gifts," the safest choice is to believe the host and leave it at that.

## A simple way to pick your number

If you're not sure, try this three-step check:

1. **Start with the relationship.** Use the table above as a starting point: about $25 for a neighbor or coworker, about $50 for a close friend and about $75 for family.
2. **Check your budget.** If that amount would squeeze your groceries or bills, go lower. No good host wants a guest to overspend.
3. **Think about the season.** If you also have weddings, birthdays or holidays coming up, spread your money across all of them. A yearly [gift fund that covers birthdays and other occasions](https://monee-app.com/blog/birthday-budget-set-a-yearly-gift-fund-without-guessing/) helps a housewarming feel like a planned expense instead of a surprise.

## Ideas at every price point

The Emily Post Institute suggests [practical, home-friendly gifts](https://emilypost.substack.com/p/etiquette-today-all-about-hosting) such as guest towels, houseplants, glasses, dish towels, picture frames, specialty foods like good olive oil or preserves, and cheeseboards or cheese knives. Here's how those ideas and a few others fit different budgets. The price ranges are rough estimates and will vary by store and country.

**Under $25**
- A potted herb or small houseplant
- Nice dish towels or hand soap
- Homemade baked goods or jam
- A local coffee or tea sampler

**$25 to $50**
- A good bottle of olive oil with specialty salt
- A picture frame with a photo of the new place or of you and the host
- A sturdy cutting board
- A set of glasses

**$50 to $75 and up**
- A cheeseboard with cheese knives
- Quality guest towels
- A larger plant or a nice planter
- A gift card to a home-improvement or furniture store

According to the same [Ally survey](https://ally.com/stories/spend/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-gift-for-any-occasion), 51% of respondents said they'd rather get an actual item for a housewarming, 33% preferred a gift card and 16% preferred cash. A physical gift is a safe default. A gift card works well too, especially for someone who is still furnishing. If you get gift cards yourself, it helps to [keep track of their balances so none go to waste](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-gift-cards-and-store-credits-without-losing-money/).

## Spend less without feeling cheap

- **Make it personal.** A handwritten note about the new chapter often means more than the price tag.
- **Pool your money.** Going in with friends lets everyone give something bigger while each person pays less. A [simple split rule for group gifts](https://monee-app.com/blog/group-gifts-without-stress-one-simple-split-rule/) keeps it fair and avoids awkward conversations.
- **Offer your time.** Help with unpacking, hanging shelves or painting a room is very useful to someone who just moved in.
- **Buy ahead.** If you see a nice serving bowl or planter on sale, buy it and keep it for the next housewarming invitation.

## Bottom line

For most housewarmings, about $25 to $50 is plenty, and $75 is generous for family or a very close friend. Gifts are optional, so let your relationship and your budget set the amount. Peer pressure shouldn't. A small, thoughtful gift and showing up to celebrate are more than enough.
