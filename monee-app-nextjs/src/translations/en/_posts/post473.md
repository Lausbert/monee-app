**Most people spend $25 to $75 on a housewarming gift, and about $40 is typical.** In a US survey by Ally Bank, the median was **$25 for acquaintances, $50 for close friends and $75 for family members**. A gift is optional, so a small, thoughtful present is perfectly polite. You don't need to spend a lot to show you care.

Below you'll find amounts for each relationship, including parents and grandparents. There's also guidance on gift cards and homemade treats, and a look at when it's worth spending more.

## Average cost of a housewarming gift

Ally, a US bank, [asked 1,000 people about their gifting habits](https://ally.com/stories/spend/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-gift-for-any-occasion) in a 2023 survey. It published the results in February 2024. For housewarmings, the typical (median) spend was **$40**. People at the higher end (the 75th percentile) spent about **$75**.

Spending also changed with the relationship:

| Relationship to host | Typical housewarming spend (Ally survey, US, USD) |
|---|---|
| Acquaintance, coworker or neighbor | $25 |
| Close friend | $50 |
| Family member | $75 |

Ally says these figures are medians and 75th percentiles "rounded up to the nearest $5." It shares few other details about how it ran the survey. I couldn't find a newer survey that measures housewarming spending specifically, so treat these numbers as a reasonable reference point, not a rule. If you live outside the US, scale the idea to local prices. A modest, practical gift is the norm almost everywhere.

## How much to spend on a housewarming gift for mom, dad or grandparents

The Ally survey uses one "family member" category, with a median of about $75. It doesn't give separate figures for parents or grandparents. In practice:

- **For your mom or dad:** Around $50 to $75 lines up with the family median. Spend more only if you want to and can afford it. For example, you might buy something they'll use every day, such as a quality planter or kitchen tool.
- **For grandparents:** The same family range works. A downsizing move may mean less space, so useful or personal gifts often go over better than more things to store. Think framed family photos, a nice plant, help with unpacking or a home-cooked meal.
- **From kids or teens:** A drawing, card or homemade treat is plenty. Nobody expects a child to match an adult's budget.

## Etiquette: is a gift even required?

No. The Emily Post Institute says the point of a housewarming is to [warm the house with the presence of people you know and your new neighbors](https://emilypost.substack.com/p/etiquette-today-all-about-hosting). If guests bring gifts, "that's lovely, but it's not required," and hosts shouldn't set up a registry or post a wish list. Emily Post says that if a guest asks the host directly, it's fine for the host to name a few things.

In practice, many guests still like to bring something small. If money is tight, a modest gift or some homemade food is perfectly polite. If the invitation says "no gifts," the safest choice is to believe the host and leave it at that. If you're only stopping by for a meal at someone's new place, the norms for a [dinner party host gift](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-dinner-party-host-gift/) are a useful comparison.

## A simple way to pick your number

If you're not sure, try this three-step check:

1. **Start with the relationship.** Use the table above as a starting point: about $25 for a neighbor or coworker, about $50 for a close friend and about $75 for family.
2. **Check your budget.** If that amount would squeeze your groceries or bills, go lower. No good host wants a guest to overspend.
3. **Think about the season.** If you also have weddings, birthdays, [baby showers](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-baby-shower-gift/) or holidays coming up, spread your money across all of them. A yearly [gift fund that covers birthdays and other occasions](https://monee-app.com/blog/birthday-budget-set-a-yearly-gift-fund-without-guessing/) helps a housewarming feel like a planned expense instead of a surprise.

## Budget vs. splurge housewarming gifts

The Emily Post Institute suggests [practical, home-friendly gifts](https://emilypost.substack.com/p/etiquette-today-all-about-hosting): guest towels, a houseplant, a patio or garden plant, glasses, dish towels, a picture frame, specialty foods like good olive oil or preserves, and a cheeseboard or cheese knives. Here's how those ideas and a few others fit different budgets. The price ranges are rough estimates and will vary by store and country.

**Budget: under $25**
- A potted herb or small houseplant
- Nice dish towels or hand soap
- Homemade baked goods or jam
- A local coffee or tea sampler
- For a neighbor new to town, a welcome kit with local restaurant menus, the town paper and transit schedules (an Emily Post idea)

**Mid-range: $25 to $50**
- A good bottle of olive oil with specialty salt
- A picture frame with a photo of the new place or of you and the host
- A sturdy cutting board
- A set of glasses

**Splurge: $50 to $75 and up**
- A cheeseboard with cheese knives
- Quality guest towels
- A larger plant or a nice planter
- A gift card to a home-improvement or furniture store

When should you splurge? It usually makes sense for close family, a first home, or a group gift where everyone chips in. A budget gift is the right choice for a casual open house, a coworker or a neighbor. It's also right any time a bigger gift would strain your finances.

## How much to put on a housewarming gift card

According to the [Ally survey](https://ally.com/stories/spend/how-much-should-you-spend-on-a-gift-for-any-occasion), 51% of respondents would rather get an actual item for a housewarming, 33% preferred a gift card and 16% preferred cash. Housewarmings were one of the two occasions, along with baby showers, where most people preferred a physical gift over money.

If you choose a gift card, the same relationship amounts are a sensible guide: about $25 for an acquaintance, $50 for a close friend and $75 for family. A card to a hardware, home-goods or furniture store works well for someone who is still furnishing. Pairing a smaller card with a plant or a handwritten note can make it feel more personal. If you get gift cards yourself, it helps to [keep track of their balances so none go to waste](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-gift-cards-and-store-credits-without-losing-money/).

## How much to spend on a housewarming cookie gift

Homemade cookies or other baked goods are a classic budget housewarming gift. If you bake them yourself, you mostly pay for ingredients and packaging, which usually keeps you at the low end of the under-$25 range. If you buy them from a bakery, a box that costs about the same as your relationship's gift amount is plenty. For a neighbor or acquaintance, that means around $25 or less. Add a note or a nice tin the host can keep if you want the gift to feel a bit more special.

## Spend less without feeling cheap

- **Make it personal.** A handwritten note about the new chapter often means more than the price tag.
- **Pool your money.** Going in with friends lets everyone give something bigger while each person pays less. A [simple split rule for group gifts](https://monee-app.com/blog/group-gifts-without-stress-one-simple-split-rule/) keeps it fair and avoids awkward conversations.
- **Offer your time.** Help with unpacking, hanging shelves or painting a room is very useful to someone who just moved in.
- **Buy ahead.** If you see a nice serving bowl or planter on sale, buy it and keep it for the next housewarming invitation.

## Bottom line

For most housewarmings in the US, about $25 to $50 is plenty, and around $75 is generous for family, including parents or grandparents. Gift cards can follow the same amounts, and homemade cookies are a perfectly good budget option. Gifts are optional, so let your relationship and your budget set the amount. Peer pressure shouldn't. A small, thoughtful gift and showing up to celebrate are more than enough.
