**To ask for a partial refund, contact the seller in writing if you can. Say what you bought, what was promised, and what went wrong. Then ask for a specific amount or percentage back.** Stay short, factual and friendly. You don't need to sound angry or apologetic. You need to be clear.

Here's the simplest script to start with:

> "Hi, I'm reaching out about order [number]. My experience didn't fully match what was described: [specific issue]. I'm not asking for a full refund, but I'd like to request a partial refund of [amount or %]. Could you help me with that?"

That's enough to begin. The rest of this guide covers what a partial refund is, how to pick a fair number, scripts for each channel, and what to do if the answer is no.

## What a Partial Refund Means

A partial refund means you get back **part** of what you paid. You usually keep the product, or you keep the part of the service you already received. It fits the middle ground: the service was late, the product worked but not as promised, or you used some of it. A full refund feels like too much, but paying full price feels wrong.

**Can you ask for a refund at all?** Yes. Asking is always allowed. Whether the company *has to* say yes depends on its policy and on the law where you live. Requests are routine. In the US, retailers expected **15.8% of 2025 annual sales** to be returned, about $849.9 billion, according to the [National Retail Federation and Happy Returns 2025 report](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025). In their 2024 survey, **67% of consumers** said a negative return experience would discourage them from shopping with a retailer again ([NRF, 2024](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/nrf-and-happy-returns-report-2024-retail-returns-total-890-billion)). Companies know that how they handle refunds matters.

**In some places, a partial refund is a legal right.** In the UK, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 lets consumers ask for a "price reduction" when a service isn't done with reasonable care and skill. Usually the trader first gets a chance to redo the work ([legislation.gov.uk, s.56](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/56)). Citizens Advice says you can go straight to a discount if redoing the service isn't possible, would take too long, or would be very inconvenient for you. You and the trader have to agree on the amount, so suggest a figure and explain why ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/somethings-gone-wrong-with-a-purchase/complain-about-a-service/)).

## Before You Ask, Get Your Facts Ready

Take five quiet minutes before you contact support. Write down:

- What you bought, plus the order number and date
- What you expected (quote the listing, ad or contract if you can)
- What actually happened
- The outcome you want: a partial refund, a waived fee, a reduced rate or a changed plan
- Your proof: receipt, photos, screenshots, delivery dates

If you track your spending with a tool like Monee, it can help here. You can say, "I looked at my spending and noticed I was charged [amount] for [service/product], but the experience didn't match what I paid for." It sounds calm because it's factual.

Check the company's refund policy and its deadlines too. Return windows close quietly, so it helps to [track return deadlines so you never lose a refund](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-track-return-deadlines-so-you-never-lose-a-refund/). If you want your money back in full instead, this [calm 3-step script for requesting a refund](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-request-a-refund-a-calm-3-step-script-that-works/) is a better fit. If the item simply dropped in price after you bought it, [ask for a price adjustment](https://monee-app.com/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-price-adjustment-after-you-buy/) instead.

**One US rule worth knowing:** the FTC's Cooling-Off Rule gives you until midnight of the third business day to cancel some sales for a full refund. It covers sales made at your home ($25 or more) or at temporary locations such as fairs or hotel rooms ($130 or more). It does **not** cover sales made entirely online, by mail or by phone ([FTC Consumer Advice](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buyers-remorse-ftcs-cooling-rule-may-help)).

## How to Choose a Fair Amount

A specific number is easier to approve than "something back." Tie it to the gap between what was promised and what you got:

- **Part of the order missing or broken:** ask for the price of that part.
- **Service partly delivered:** ask for the share that wasn't delivered.
- **Late or lower quality, but usable:** a modest percentage is easier to accept than a big one.

*Hypothetical example:* you booked a 4-day course for $400 and one day was cancelled without a make-up session. Asking for $100 (one quarter) is easy to explain. Asking for $300 isn't.

## Email Script for a Partial Refund

**Subject line options:**

- Request for partial refund on order [order number]
- Partial refund request: [product/service], [purchase date]

**Email script:**

Hi [Name/Support Team],

I'm contacting you about [product/service/order number], purchased on [date].

I expected [what was promised or described], but [brief, specific issue]. Because I did receive/use part of it, I'm not asking for a full refund.

I'd like to request a partial refund of [amount or %] to my original payment method. I've attached [photos/screenshots/receipt] for context.

Could you please review this and let me know what you can do?

Thank you,
[Your Name]

Keep it short. Long emotional emails make the issue harder to process. Specific beats intense.

## Phone Script

Start with this:

"Hi, I'm calling about a purchase from [date], order [number]. I'd like help with a partial refund because the product/service didn't fully match what was described."

If they ask what happened:

"The issue is [one-sentence explanation]. I did receive/use part of it, so I'm not asking for a full refund. I'm asking for [amount] back for the part that didn't meet the description."

If they ask what you want:

"I'd like [outcome]. Is that something you're able to approve?"

If they say they can't:

"I understand. Is there a supervisor or customer care team who can review exceptions?"

Speak slowly. You don't need to fill the silence. At the end, ask for a reference number and write down the agent's name and the date.

## Chat Script

Hi, I need help with order [order number]. The product/service was partly useful, but [specific issue] did not match what was described. I'm requesting a partial refund of [amount] rather than a full refund. Can you review this?

If the chat agent sends a policy reply:

Thanks. I understand the standard policy. I'm asking whether an exception or adjustment is possible because [specific issue]. Could you escalate this for review?

Chat gives you a written record automatically. Save the transcript.

## If They Say X, You Say Y

**They say:** "We don't offer refunds."
**You say:** "I understand that may be the standard policy. I'm asking for a partial adjustment because the service/product did not match [specific promise]. What options are available?"

**They say:** "You already used it."
**You say:** "That's why I'm not asking for a full refund. I'm asking for a partial refund for the part that didn't work as described."

**They say:** "There's nothing we can do."
**You say:** "Could you please escalate this to someone who can review exceptions?"

**They offer store credit only:**
**You say:** "Thank you. I'd prefer a refund to my original payment method. Is that possible?" (In the UK, a price reduction for a service must go back by the same payment method unless you agree otherwise, under [CRA s.56](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/56).)

**They offer less than you asked for:**
**You say:** "I appreciate the offer. Given [specific issue], would you be able to increase that to [amount]?"

## Asking for a Refund on Someone Else's Behalf

You can help a parent, partner or friend ask for a refund. Expect the company to want the account holder involved, though, because it can't usually discuss an account with a stranger. The smoothest approach is to make the call together, or to have the account holder send the request from their own email. You can draft the message for them using the scripts above. If you write for them, say so up front: "I'm writing on behalf of [name], the account holder, who is copied on this email."

## If the First Try Doesn't Work

Wait a business day or two, then follow up in writing.

Subject: Follow-up on partial refund request for [order number]

Hi [Name/Support Team],

I'm following up on my request from [date] (reference [number], if any). I'm asking for a partial refund of [amount] because [specific issue]. I'd appreciate a review by a supervisor or customer care specialist.

Thank you,
[Your Name]

If that doesn't work, look at how you paid:

- **Credit card (US):** under the Fair Credit Billing Act, you can dispute charges for goods or services you didn't accept or that weren't delivered as agreed. Your letter must reach the card issuer within 60 days after the first bill with the error was sent to you. For quality problems, you must first try to resolve the issue with the seller, and other conditions apply ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/using-credit-cards-and-disputing-charges)).
- **Buy now, pay later (US):** in 2024 the CFPB issued a rule treating some BNPL loans like credit cards for disputes and refunds. It **withdrew that rule on May 12, 2025** ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/consumer-cards-resources/buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-products/)). Check your BNPL provider's own dispute process, and keep making payments while it's reviewed. If you're weighing BNPL for a future booking, this [refund-risk and due-date checklist](https://monee-app.com/blog/should-you-use-buy-now-pay-later-for-travel-a-refundrisk-duedate-checklist/) helps.
- **UK services:** Citizens Advice suggests alternative dispute resolution as a next step, before going to court ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/somethings-gone-wrong-with-a-purchase/complain-about-a-service/)).

You're not being difficult. You're pointing out the gap between what was promised and what was delivered, and asking for a fair adjustment. Being calm, specific and documented is enough.
